The problem

Most tickets are already answered.

A large share of what arrives at the service desk already has a knowledge base article covering it. People don't find the article — they search, give up, and open a ticket. Every one of those is a question the desk answers twice.

Ask a general AI instead and it will answer confidently with settings from some other university, because it has no USask articles in front of it and no way to tell you that.

How it works

Retrieve first. Then answer.

We index the IT knowledge base, split articles into passages, and find the few that actually match the question. Only those go to the model, with an instruction to use nothing else.

"My account keeps getting locked"
Accounts lock after several failed sign-in attempts. The most common cause is a device still holding an old password and retrying in the background. Update the saved password on every device before signing in again.

The part nobody else builds

It knows what to escalate.

Search always returns something. Hand a model four irrelevant passages and it will still try to help — and a wrong instruction about MFA or account recovery locks someone out entirely.

"Can you reset my password for me?"
I can explain how to reset it yourself, but I can't make changes to your account or verify your identity. For a reset you can't do yourself, open a ticket with the service desk.

Tier zero is the self-service layer. It resolves what it can and hands off cleanly what it can't — escalation is the design, not a failure. Where we draw that line is a measured decision, not a guess.
